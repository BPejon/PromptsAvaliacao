## Overall Assessment

The survey addresses a broad and appropriate range of NMR applications in upstream oil and gas, from laboratory petrophysics and EOR evaluation to unconventional rock characterization and LWD/geosteering. It is useful as a topic map and contains relevant case studies and summary tables. However, the manuscript is seriously undermined by editorial and citation problems, internal notation and equation inconsistencies, unresolved cross-references, and a tendency in several sections to summarize papers sequentially rather than synthesize them. The scientific scope is relevant, but the reliability and polish of the presentation are below what would be expected for a rigorous review.

---

## 1. Coverage

**Score: 4**

**Critical observations:**  
The survey covers most major areas required by its stated scope, including NMR theory, laboratory petrophysical measurements, EOR applications, drilling/completion-related topics, unconventional reservoirs, and field-scale LWD/geosteering. Representative developments are discussed, and several recent or advanced techniques such as 2D NMR, T1–T2, T2–D, and T2–T2 are introduced.

**Evidence:**  
- Sections on porosity, pore size distribution, permeability, fluid saturation, capillary pressure, and wettability provide direct coverage of core NMR petrophysics.
- EOR subsection reviews CO2, surfactant, polymer, and nano-surfactant applications.
- Unconventional rock characterization includes shale, kerogen, bitumen, and solid-state NMR techniques.
- Field-scale section reviews LWD tool evolution, tar detection, and geosteering case studies.

**Limitations:**  
Some parts are thinner than others—for example, wireline NMR applications receive much less detailed treatment than LWD, and field-scale quantitative interpretation examples are limited. However, relative to the declared scope, the coverage is broad enough to warrant a good score.

## 2. Relevance

**Score: 4**

**Critical observations:**  
The substantive content is strongly aligned with the survey’s stated purpose. Background material on NMR physics is extensive but mostly necessary for understanding the later applications. Minor digressions exist, such as brief references to lithium-ion battery electrolytes and downstream NMR applications, but these are short and framed as evidence of NMR’s broader energy-industry relevance.

**Evidence:**  
- The theory section establishes relaxation, diffusion, and 2D measurements needed for petrophysical interpretation.
- The special-topics subsections on mud filtrate invasion and emulsion droplet sizing are clearly connected to drilling/production applications.
- Unconventional and field-scale sections remain within the upstream scope.

**Limitations:**  
A few passages feel generic or introductory, but they do not substantially reduce focus.

## 3. Structure

**Score: 3**

**Critical observations:**  
The overall organization is conceptually logical: theory, laboratory applications, petrophysics, EOR, special topics, unconventional rocks, field-scale logging, future directions, and conclusions. However, structural execution is uneven. Cross-references are broken, equation numbering is disordered, and some headings or transitions are duplicated or unclear.

**Evidence:**  
- Equation numbering is inconsistent: equations related to diffusion/APGSTE appear out of sequence around the 2D NMR discussion, disrupting the logical progression.
- The section title appears as “NMR applications in field scaleNMR technology has been widely used...”, indicating a duplicated or malformed heading.
- Multiple figure references appear as “Error! Reference source not found.”
- Table 4 is referenced in the wettability discussion but is not visible in the provided text.

These issues do not destroy the overall framework, but they weaken the developmental coherence of the survey.

## 4. Synthesis

**Score: 3**

**Critical observations:**  
The survey partly organizes the literature by application and provides some useful comparative observations, such as NMR advantages over macroscopic EOR screening methods and comparisons of NMR-derived porosity with gravimetric or MICP results. However, many subsections remain mostly paper-by-paper summaries rather than analytical integration.

**Evidence:**  
- Tables 2 and 3 provide useful overviews, but they are largely descriptive summaries rather than frameworks exposing trade-offs or conceptual relationships.
- The EOR section reports several studies in sequence: Mitchell et al., Al Harbi et al., Dong et al., Mamoudou et al., and Zhu et al., but does not develop a strong comparative framework.
- Field application case studies are listed individually rather than synthesized into general geosteering or LWD interpretation principles.

**Limitations:**  
Meaningful categories exist, but deeper analysis of relationships, contradictions, gaps, or design trade-offs is limited.

## 5. Accuracy & Evidence

**Score: 2**

**Critical observations:**  
There are multiple internal inconsistencies, unsupported or unresolved statements, and apparent equation errors. Some broad claims are stronger than the evidence presented, and placeholders remain in the text.

**Evidence of problems:**  
- Notation is inconsistent: the longitudinal relaxation time is written as \(T_f\), \(T_I\), and \(T_1\) in different places; elsewhere \(T_j\) appears.
- Equation 7 is presented as \(\| M - K F\| + a\| F\|^2\), but the data-misfit term should normally be squared for Tikhonov regularization.
- Equation 21 gives  
  \[
  \frac{1}{T_2}\approx \rho_2^2 \left(\frac{S}{V}\right),
  \]
  but the expected form would be linear in \(\rho_2\), as used elsewhere.
- The text says “The only variable in Eq. 8 is the applied gradient strength,” but Eq. 8 is the phase-shift equation, not the signal-attenuation equation.
- The survey contains unresolved placeholders such as “provide reference here please” and multiple “Error! Reference source not found” entries.
- Table 4 is cited but not present, making the wettability-index discussion incomplete.
- Some abstract claims, such as NMR being “better than the current techniques,” are stated broadly and are only partially substantiated by the reviewed evidence.

These issues are frequent enough to reduce confidence in the accuracy and editorial reliability of the survey.

## 6. Citation Integrity

**Score: 2**

**Critical observations:**  
Citation practice is inconsistent and partly unreliable. Many citations are provided, but there are clear mismatches between in-text citations and the visible reference list, duplicated references, year-suffix inconsistencies, and unresolved missing citations.

**Evidence:**  
- Several in-text works appear not to have corresponding reference entries in the provided bibliography, such as Ellis and Singer (2007), Liu (2017), Lalanne and Rebelle (2014), DePavia et al. (2003), Freedman et al. (2003), and Hurlimann and Heaton (2015).
- In-text suffixes do not always match the reference list: for example, “Adebayo and Bageri, 2020a” is used, but the listed reference appears without a suffix; similarly, “Bageri et al., 2021b” is cited, while only an undifferentiated 2021 entry is visible.
- Some references appear duplicated, such as two very similar Sjöblom et al. (2017) entries and repeated Mitchell et al. (2014a/b) entries with the same title.
- The visible bibliography contains garbled or malformed entries, making it difficult to associate some in-text citations with their intended sources.
- The explicit placeholder “provide reference here please” indicates a missing citation where one is clearly required.

These problems are substantial and affect the traceability and integrity of the review’s evidence base.

## 7. Writing Quality & Editorial Consistency

**Score: 2**

**Critical observations:**  
The prose is generally understandable, but frequent typographical, terminological, and formatting inconsistencies reduce readability and give the impression of an insufficiently edited manuscript.

**Evidence:**  
- Inconsistent relaxation-time notation (\(T_f\), \(T_I\), \(T_1\), \(T_j\)).
- Numerous typographical errors, e.g., “pervious conditions,” “restauration,” “the first trail,” “filed” instead of “field.”
- Duplicate or malformed section headings, as in “NMR applications in field scaleNMR technology...”
- Broken cross-references and missing table/figure references.
- Some reference-list entries are visually merged together, making the bibliography difficult to parse.
- Placeholder text such as “provide reference here please” was left in the final version.

These issues are frequent enough to impair readability and editorial professionalism, though the underlying technical content remains mostly comprehensible.
