## Evaluation Notes

**Overall assessment:** The survey addresses a broad and relevant topic, covering NMR theory, laboratory petrophysical applications, EOR, drilling-related topics, unconventional reservoir characterization, and field-scale logging-while-drilling/geosteering. Its main strengths are the range of applications discussed and the attempt to integrate laboratory and field perspectives. However, the manuscript is substantially weakened by editorial and internal consistency problems, including broken cross-references, missing or incomplete tables, equation errors, inconsistent notation, and citation irregularities. Many sections are descriptive and case-study-oriented rather than analytically synthesized.

### 1. Coverage

**Score:** 4

**Critical observations:** The survey covers most major areas required by its stated scope: NMR relaxation/diffusion theory, core-analysis petrophysics, EOR applications, drilling-induced damage, emulsion sizing, unconventional shale characterization, field logging tools, tar detection, and geosteering. Representative studies and commercial tools are included. Coverage is therefore broad and generally appropriate. Some imbalance exists: laboratory-scale applications are more developed than field-scale applications, and several field-scale discussions are hardware summaries rather than application-level reviews. However, this does not substantially undermine the survey’s stated purpose.

**Evidence:** Sections on porosity, pore size distribution, permeability, saturation, capillary pressure, and wettability are present and supported by references; field applications include LWD tool development and field cases such as tar detection and geosteering.

### 2. Relevance

**Score:** 4

**Critical observations:** Virtually all major sections support the central scope of NMR applications in oil and gas. The NMR theory section is extensive but is necessary background for the later applications. Special topics such as emulsion droplet sizing and mud-filtrate invasion are clearly connected to drilling/production applications. Some portions, especially in the future directions section, are slightly generic, but they remain tied to NMR research needs.

**Evidence:** The theory section prepares the reader for relaxation, diffusion, and 2D NMR discussions; the drilling, EOR, and unconventional sections all return explicitly to NMR-based interpretation.

### 3. Structure

**Score:** 3

**Critical observations:** The overall organization is logical, moving from theory to laboratory applications, special topics, unconventional characterization, field tools, and field cases. However, internal organization is uneven. Some field-scale subsections proceed as paper-by-paper or tool-by-tool summaries, and transitions between studies are weak. There are clear structural/editorial defects, such as the duplicated heading “NMR applications in field scaleNMR technology…” and equation numbering that jumps from Eq. 8 to Eq. 12 and later from Eq. 27 to Eq. 29.

**Evidence:** Section 4 heading appears merged with its first sentence; Eq. 12 is introduced before Eqs. 10–11; Eq. 29 appears after Eq. 27 without Eq. 28.

### 4. Synthesis

**Score:** 3

**Critical observations:** The survey provides some meaningful grouping by application area and includes comparative content, such as the discussion of permeability models and the summary table for porosity studies. However, much of the EOR and field-case material is presented study by study without strong analytical integration. Table 2, which is intended to summarize 2D NMR measurement types and applications, is incomplete and therefore does not function as a useful synthesis tool. The discussion of common interpretation pitfalls is one of the more integrative parts.

**Evidence:** Table 3 summarizes porosity studies across lithologies, and permeability models are compared; by contrast, many LWD tool developments and field cases are described sequentially rather than integrated into a comparative framework.

### 5. Accuracy & Evidence

**Score:** 2

**Critical observations:** There are multiple substantive internal inconsistencies and apparent errors. Eq. 21 gives \(1/T_2 \approx \rho_2^2 (S/V)\), which is inconsistent with surrounding Eqs. 19–20 where surface relaxivity appears as \(\rho_2\), not \(\rho_2^2\). The text states that the only variable in Eq. 8 is gradient strength, but Eq. 8 is a phase-shift expression, not the attenuation equation. The inversion recovery expression in Eq. 13 uses \(1-\exp(\tau_1/T_1)\), conflicting with the earlier Eq. 2 form \(1-2\exp(-\tau_1/T_1)\). The manuscript also contains unresolved placeholder text such as “provide reference here please” and repeated “Error! Reference source not found” instead of figure references.

**Evidence:** Eq. 21 versus Eqs. 19–20; the discussion following Eq. 8; Eq. 13 versus Eq. 2; multiple unresolved cross-references in the unconventional-reservoir section.

### 6. Citation Integrity

**Score:** 2

**Critical observations:** Citation practice is mixed and includes serious internal irregularities. An in-text citation to “Dong et al., 2020b” appears without a corresponding reference in the supplied reference list. The reference list contains a corrupted entry for “Tester F, Deviated H, Rio K…” that is not a normal bibliographic record. Duplicate entries also appear, such as repeated Mitchell et al. 2014a/2014b references with the same title “Monitoring chemical EOR processes,” and repeated Sjoblom et al. 2017 entries. Multiple in-text citation suffixes, such as “Freedman et al., 2003a” and “Chen et al. 2018a, b,” are ambiguous in relation to the available reference list.

**Evidence:** Missing Dong et al. reference; corrupted Tester et al. reference; duplicated Mitchell and Sjoblom entries; ambiguous lettered citations.

### 7. Writing Quality & Editorial Consistency

**Score:** 2

**Critical observations:** The manuscript contains frequent typographical, terminological, and editorial inconsistencies that impair readability. The notation \(T_f\) is used inconsistently for longitudinal relaxation instead of \(T_1\). There are repeated spelling errors such as “earth filed,” “filed test,” and “stationery”; author names are inconsistently rendered, e.g., “Oguntona” versus “Oquntona.” The manuscript refers to a “Table 4” that is not present, and Table 2 is incomplete. The unresolved cross-references and placeholder text further indicate inadequate editorial quality.

**Evidence:** Duplicated and merged section heading; “provide reference here please”; “Error! Reference source not found”; missing Table 4; inconsistent \(T_f\)/\(T_1\) notation; multiple typographical errors throughout.
