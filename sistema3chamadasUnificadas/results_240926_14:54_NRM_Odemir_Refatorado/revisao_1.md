## Overall Assessment

The survey provides a broad and generally well-organized overview of NMR applications in the oil and gas industry, covering fundamentals, petrophysical applications, EOR monitoring, unconventional reservoirs, and advanced techniques. Its main strengths are its wide topical coverage and several useful explanatory passages, especially around relaxation mechanisms, pore size interpretation, and fluid typing. However, the manuscript is weakened by uneven synthesis, internal inconsistencies in technical discussion, citation irregularities, and occasional inclusion of peripheral non-oil-and-gas examples. It reads as a competent but not fully polished or critically integrated review.

## 1. Coverage

**Score:** 4

**Critical observations:** The survey covers the major areas expected from its stated scope: NMR fundamentals, petrophysics, EOR, unconventional reservoirs, and advanced/future techniques. The coverage is broad and generally balanced. However, some topics emphasized in the abstract or framing are underdeveloped, particularly logging while drilling, thermal EOR monitoring, and machine learning/AI integration.

**Evidence:** The abstract states that LWD integration is discussed, but the main text only treats LWD briefly under future directions. Section 4.3 on thermal EOR is thin and even acknowledges that “specific details on steam injection monitoring are limited.” Machine learning and AI are discussed only as emerging possibilities rather than as a substantive part of the reviewed literature.

## 2. Relevance

**Score:** 4

**Critical observations:** Most content directly supports the survey’s stated purpose of reviewing NMR for oil and gas applications. The fundamentals sections are necessary and generally well motivated. However, several illustrative examples are drawn from outside the oil and gas domain and are only loosely connected to the main scope.

**Evidence:** Figure 1 uses sea cucumber CPMG data, Figure 15 uses yeast cells for high-field NMR, and Section 6.3 discusses surface NMR for groundwater and glacial water mapping. These examples are tied back to NMR principles, but they dilute the petroleum focus and are not essential to the review.

## 3. Structure

**Score:** 4

**Critical observations:** The overall organization is logical: fundamentals, petrophysical applications, EOR monitoring, unconventional reservoirs, advanced techniques, and future directions. Transitions are generally clear. Some structural weaknesses exist where topics introduced in the abstract are not developed in a dedicated section.

**Evidence:** LWD is mentioned prominently in the abstract and conclusion but lacks a corresponding substantive section. Surface NMR is placed under NMR Imaging / MRI, although it is really a surface geophysics method and is not strongly linked back to oil and gas reservoir characterization.

## 4. Synthesis

**Score:** 3

**Critical observations:** The survey does more than simple enumeration in places. It groups related applications, compares permeability models, discusses pore-size dependent recovery in EOR, and uses T1–T2 maps as a conceptual framework for fluid typing. However, much of the synthesis remains uneven and relatively shallow, with many sections reading as descriptive summaries rather than critical integration.

**Evidence:** Section 3.3 compares Timur-Coates and SDR permeability models, but the trade-offs and practical calibration issues are not deeply analyzed. Section 5.2 on tight gas sands largely lists NMR capabilities and findings without integrating them into a larger interpretive framework. Future directions are presented as broad themes rather than conclusions derived from a systematic gap analysis.

## 5. Accuracy & Evidence

**Score:** 3

**Critical observations:** The survey is generally plausible and internally coherent, but it contains noticeable technical inconsistencies and overstated claims. Some equations and discussions contain contradictory or confusing statements that reduce confidence in the precision of the presentation.

**Evidence:** The SDR permeability model discussion is internally contradictory: the displayed Equation (18) uses $\phi^4 T_{2,\text{gm}}^2$, but the accompanying note states that “the provided text uses $\phi^2 T_{2,\text{gm}}^2$” and then says the more common form is $\phi^4$. Section 6.2 defines high-field NMR as typically above 3 T, but later refers to 22 MHz systems as high-field, which is not consistent with that definition for protons. The abstract also claims LWD integration is discussed, though the main text provides only limited future-oriented treatment.

## 6. Citation Integrity

**Score:** 3

**Critical observations:** Citation practice is mixed. The survey includes many references and generally cites major claims, but there are duplicate references, inconsistent citation styles, and some apparent mismatches between in-text attributions and the reference list.

**Evidence:** References [5] and [67] are identical, as are [14] and [52]. The text mixes numeric citations with author-year citations, such as “Seevers (1966)” and “Banavar and Schwartz (1987),” but not all of these appear in the reference list. Figure 1 is attributed to Hriberšek 2024 [13], but the listed reference is a preface in *Clotho*, which is internally inconsistent with the described CPMG figure. Some listed references, such as [179] and [180], do not appear to be cited in the body.

## 7. Writing Quality & Editorial Consistency

**Score:** 3

**Critical observations:** The prose is generally clear and readable, but editorial quality is uneven. There are noticeable inconsistencies in citation formatting, terminology, and figure integration. Some passages appear to include meta-commentary or drafting artifacts rather than polished academic prose.

**Evidence:** The SDR permeability paragraph includes an editorial aside about what “the provided text uses,” which is inappropriate in a finished review. Citation style alternates between numeric and author-year formats. Terminology is not always consistent, and several sections, especially in the heavy oil and oil sands subsection, are dense and repetitive. These issues do not make the survey unreadable, but they weaken its professional consistency.
