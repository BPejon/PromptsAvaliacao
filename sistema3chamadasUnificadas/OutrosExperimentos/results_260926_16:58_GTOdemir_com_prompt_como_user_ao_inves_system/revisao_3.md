## Overall Assessment

The survey addresses a broad and appropriate set of NMR applications in oil and gas, from laboratory petrophysics and EOR to unconventional rocks and LWD field operations. Its main strengths are topical breadth, useful summaries of NMR methods, and attention to practical issues such as surface relaxivity, internal gradients, formation damage, and tool design. However, the manuscript is seriously undermined by inconsistent notation, broken cross-references, apparent equation errors, missing or mismatched citations, duplicated references, and a tendency in several sections to summarize individual studies rather than synthesize the literature.

## Coverage

**Score:** 4

**Critical observations:**  
The survey covers most major areas required by its stated scope: NMR theory, relaxation/diffusion/2D methods, porosity, pore size distribution, permeability, saturation, capillary pressure, wettability, EOR, drilling-fluid damage, emulsions, unconventional rock characterization, LWD tool design, geosteering, and field applications. The balance is generally reasonable, and the survey does not merely enumerate topics; it provides substantive explanations of key principles and representative applications.

**Evidence:**  
- Dedicated sections on petrophysical properties and EOR include multiple studies and practical limitations.  
- Unconventional shale characterization is treated in detail with discussion of kerogen maturity, organic/inorganic pores, internal gradients, and solid-state NMR.  
- Table 3 summarizes porosity studies across different lithologies, and Table 2 lists 2D NMR measurement types.

**Weaknesses:**  
Some important areas, such as spatially resolved MRI and flow propagators, are only briefly mentioned in the future directions rather than developed earlier, and some field-scale interpretation topics are relatively thin compared with the laboratory sections.

## Relevance

**Score:** 4

**Critical observations:**  
The content strongly aligns with the stated objective of reviewing NMR applications in laboratory and field settings. Background material on NMR theory is necessary and mostly well motivated. The few peripheral examples, such as lithium-ion battery electrolytes and downstream applications, are used briefly to contextualize NMR more broadly and do not materially reduce focus.

**Evidence:**  
- The introduction uses energy-industry examples to motivate the flexibility of NMR, then returns to oil and gas applications.  
- Laboratory and field sections consistently connect back to the central goal of improving reservoir characterization, EOR evaluation, and drilling/logging operations.

**Weaknesses:**  
Some background statements in the introduction are generic, but they remain reasonably concise and relevant.

## Structure

**Score:** 3

**Critical observations:**  
The high-level organization is logical: theory, laboratory applications, special topics, field-scale applications, future directions, and conclusions. However, the internal development is uneven. Several subsections, especially field LWD and geosteering, read as case-by-case summaries rather than conceptually organized synthesis. The heading “NMR applications in field scale” is repeated awkwardly, and “Special topics” combines formation damage, emulsions, interpretation pitfalls, and unconventional rocks without a strongly articulated conceptual link.

**Evidence:**  
- The repeated heading “NMR applications in field scaleNMR technology” is an editorial structural defect.  
- Field implementation sections describe individual field cases in sequence, with limited connective framing.  
- Figures are generally integrated, but unresolved cross-references disrupt the narrative flow.

## Synthesis

**Score:** 3

**Critical observations:**  
The survey does more than pure enumeration in several places. It identifies key interpretive difficulties such as pore coupling, internal magnetic field gradients, surface relaxivity variability, and limitations of permeability models. It also compares different NMR methods and tool designs. However, much of the EOR and field-application material is organized around individual studies, with limited development of overarching frameworks, design spaces, or comparative conclusions.

**Evidence:**  
- The discussion of permeability models compares Coates, Timur, and SDR equations and notes their empirical limitations.  
- The section on common misinterpretations meaningfully links pore coupling and internal gradients to petrophysical errors.  
- By contrast, the EOR and geosteering sections often present study-by-study summaries without generalizing findings across conditions.

## Accuracy & Evidence

**Score:** 2

**Critical observations:**  
The survey contains multiple substantive internal inconsistencies, technical errors, and unsupported or overgeneralized claims. Some equations are misnumbered or internally inconsistent, and several cross-references do not resolve. The use of notation such as \(T_f\), \(T_I\), and \(T_j\) for longitudinal relaxation is inconsistent and potentially misleading.

**Evidence:**  
- Equation numbering is disordered: after Eq. 8, the text discusses Eqs. 10 and 11 before Eq. 12 appears, and the discussion of gradient strength incorrectly refers to Eq. 8.  
- Eq. 21 states \(\frac{1}{T_2} \approx \rho_2^2 \left(\frac{S}{V}\right)\), which is inconsistent with Eq. 19 and with the usual dimensional form \(\rho_2 S/V\).  
- The phrase “Error! Reference source not found” appears repeatedly, indicating unresolved figure/reference links.  
- Broad claims such as NMR being “better than the current techniques used for screening, evaluation, and assessment” in EOR are stated with more certainty than the presented evidence supports.  
- A passage in the unconventional section includes the unresolved instruction “provide reference here please,” which affects both evidence quality and editorial reliability.

## Citation Integrity

**Score:** 2

**Critical observations:**  
Citation practice is inconsistent and contains several identifiable problems. Some important claims lack citations, some in-text citations do not correspond clearly to entries in the reference list, and several references appear duplicated or ambiguous.

**Evidence:**  
- The text contains the statement “provide reference here please,” indicating a missing citation.  
- The introduction cites “Liu 2017,” but the reference list includes “Hu H (2017) Principles and applications of well logging,” suggesting an in-text/reference mismatch.  
- “Chen et al. 2018a” is cited in connection with Halliburton XMR logging hardware, but the corresponding reference title is about CO₂ injection, creating an apparent mismatch.  
- “Fleury and Romero-Sarmiento (2016a,b)” and “Dong et al. (2020b)” are cited in the text but do not clearly appear in the provided reference list.  
- “Mitchell, Jonathan, et al. (2014a)” and “(2014b)” appear to be duplicated entries with the same DOI.  
- Several citations include “a,b” suffixes in the text that are not consistently reflected in the bibliography.

## Writing Quality & Editorial Consistency

**Score:** 2

**Critical observations:**  
The manuscript is generally understandable but suffers from frequent and significant editorial problems. Terminology and notation are inconsistent, some sentences are ungrammatical or repetitive, and cross-references/formatting are unreliable. These issues are frequent enough to reduce readability and professional presentation.

**Evidence:**  
- Notation for longitudinal relaxation varies among \(T_1\), \(T_I\), \(T_f\), and \(T_j\) in different parts of the text.  
- The repeated heading “NMR applications in field scaleNMR technology” is a clear editorial error.  
- The unresolved “Error! Reference source not found” and “provide reference here please” notes indicate incomplete editing.  
- Equation numbering is nonsequential and inconsistent.  
- The reference list contains duplicated entries and inconsistent formatting of author names, journal titles, and DOIs.  
- Several sentences are awkward or repetitive, such as “This is because the disappearance of the longer component of the NMR relaxation time spectrum corresponds directly to the loss of hydrogen containing fluid from the pores due to desaturation,” followed immediately by a similar explanation.
