## Overall Assessment

The survey addresses a broad and relevant scope covering laboratory and field-scale NMR applications in the upstream oil and gas industry. Its main strength is the breadth of topics included, from petrophysics and EOR to drilling fluids, emulsions, unconventional rocks, and LWD/geosteering. However, the manuscript is substantially weakened by editorial inconsistencies, unresolved cross-references, citation-reference mismatches, and some technical equation errors. The survey is useful as a broad overview but would benefit from more critical synthesis and careful editing.

---

## 1. Accuracy & Evidence

**Score: 3**

### Critical Observations

The survey is broadly coherent and many individual descriptions are linked to citations. However, it contains noticeable overgeneralizations, internal technical inconsistencies, and unsupported conclusions.

Several claims are stated more strongly than the evidence in the survey supports, particularly in the abstract and introduction. For example, the statement that NMR “was found to be better than the current techniques used for screening, evaluation, and assessment” is a broad superiority claim that is not demonstrated by a systematic comparison in the survey. Similarly, the claim that NMR “showed a very good potential for exploration and production advancement in unconventional gas fields compared to other tools” is presented without a clear comparative evidence base.

There are also internal technical/equation inconsistencies:
- Eq. 19 expresses relaxation enhancement as proportional to \(\rho_2 S/V\), while Eq. 21 later writes the relation with \(\rho_2^2\), which is internally inconsistent if both are intended to describe the same surface relaxation term.
- The diffusion section states, “The only variable in Eq. 8 is the applied gradient strength, \(g\),” but Eq. 8 is the phase-shift equation, while Eq. 9 is the signal-attenuation equation being described.
- Equation numbering skips Eq. 28; the text jumps from Eq. 27 to Eq. 29.
- The manuscript states that NMR “has proven its robustness” in the energy industry but then says NMR “is an immature field of research,” creating an unresolved tension in framing.

These issues do not make the survey incoherent, but they reduce confidence in some detailed technical descriptions and broad conclusions.

## 2. Citation Integrity

**Score: 2**

### Critical Observations

The survey has many references and many substantive claims are cited, but citation practice is inconsistent and contains clear apparent mismatches and omissions.

Several in-text citations do not correspond to entries in the reference list:
- The introduction cites “Ellis and Singer 2007” and “Liu 2017,” but the reference list contains “Hu H (2017)” and no corresponding Ellis and Singer or Liu entry that clearly matches.
- “Fleury and Romero-Sarmiento (2016a, b)” is cited repeatedly in the text and figures, but no full reference for Fleury and Romero-Sarmiento appears in the reference list.
- “Freedman et al., 2003a” is cited in the text, but the provided reference list does not show a matching Freedman 2003a entry.
- “Mitchell et al. 2012b” is cited, but the reference list includes Mitchell et al. 2012a and 2012c without a clear 2012b entry.
- The unconventional section contains an explicit placeholder: “provide reference here please,” indicating a missing citation.

The reference list itself also contains apparent irregularities:
- “Chen Q, Balcom BJ, (2008) (12) United States Patent 2” appears to be a malformed or incomplete reference.
- Some entries appear duplicated, such as Sjoblom et al. 2017 and Mitchell et al. 2014a/2014b.
- The formatting of many references is uneven, and several entries appear truncated or inconsistently annotated.

Table 2 also compresses many applications and references into a single dense row, making it difficult to identify which reference supports which specific claim.

## 3. Writing Quality & Editorial Consistency

**Score: 2**

### Critical Observations

The writing is understandable in places but has frequent editorial and formatting problems that reduce readability and give the survey an unfinished appearance.

Common issues include:
- Unresolved cross-references: “Error! Reference source not found” appears multiple times in the text for figures and tables, particularly in the unconventional rock characterization section.
- Inconsistent notation: the manuscript alternates between \(T_1\), \(T_f\), and sometimes \(T_j\); similar inconsistencies occur for \(T_1/T_2\) and \(T_f/T_2\).
- Typographical errors and malformed headings: e.g., “NMR applications in field scaleNMR technology has been widely used...”
- Equation numbering and presentation are inconsistent, with equations inserted out of logical order and jumps in numbering.
- The explicit placeholder “provide reference here please” remains in the text.
- Reference list formatting is inconsistent, with duplicated or incomplete entries.

These are not isolated lapses; they occur frequently enough to impair the professional presentation and readability of the manuscript.

## 4. Coverage

**Score: 4**

### Critical Observations

The survey covers a wide range of relevant topics within its stated scope:
- Laboratory petrophysics: porosity, pore size distribution, permeability, saturation, capillary pressure, and wettability.
- EOR applications, including surfactant flooding, CO₂ injection, polymer flooding, and huff-and-puff.
- Specialized applications such as mud filtrate invasion, emulsion droplet sizing, pore coupling, and internal magnetic field gradients.
- Unconventional rock characterization using \(T_1-T_2\), \(T_2-D\), solid-state NMR, and high-field NMR.
- Field-scale NMR logging, LWD-NMR tool design, geosteering, and tar detection.

The coverage is representative and well aligned with the declared scope. Some areas, such as capillary pressure and wettability, are discussed but less deeply than porosity/permeability or LWD tool development. There is also some imbalance toward tool-design descriptions in the field-scale section. However, these are not severe enough to substantially compromise the survey.

## 5. Relevance

**Score: 4**

## 6. Structure

**Score: 3**

### Critical Observations

The overall organization is reasonable: introduction, NMR theory, laboratory applications, special topics, field-scale applications, future directions, and conclusions. This gives the reader a logical progression from fundamentals to applications and then to future research.

However, there are structural weaknesses:
- Figures and tables are sometimes not properly referenced, with repeated “Error! Reference source not found” placeholders.
- The “Special topics” section is a catch-all that mixes drilling fluid damage, emulsion sizing, misinterpretation in conventional rocks, and unconventional rock characterization. Some of these could be organized more coherently.
- The field-scale section is largely a sequence of tool descriptions and case studies, with limited conceptual transition between instrument design and field applications.
- Some technical material, especially equations in the diffusion and 2D NMR sections, is presented in a tangled order, making the structural progression less clear.

Thus, while the high-level outline is logical, the internal organization has noticeable weaknesses.

## 7. Synthesis

**Score: 3**

### Critical Observations

The survey provides some synthesis through thematic grouping and comparative tables:
- Table 3 summarizes NMR porosity studies across lithologies and compares limitations.
- The review groups EOR applications by method and identifies practical limitations, such as the restriction of benchtop NMR to small core samples.
- It discusses common interpretation pitfalls, such as pore coupling and internal gradients, across multiple formation types.
- The conclusions identify broad themes and future research directions.

However, much of the survey remains descriptive and paper-by-paper. Many subsections summarize individual studies without sufficient critical comparison or integration. For example, the permeability section lists models such as Timur, Coates, and SDR, but does not deeply compare their assumptions, domain applicability, or validation quality. Similarly, the field-scale applications are often presented as separate case studies rather than being integrated into a broader analysis of tool capabilities and limitations.

The survey is therefore more than a simple list, but its analytical synthesis is uneven and often shallow.
