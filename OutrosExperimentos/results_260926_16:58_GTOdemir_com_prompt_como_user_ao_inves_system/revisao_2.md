## Overall Assessment

This survey covers a broad and relevant range of NMR applications in the oil and gas industry, from laboratory petrophysics and EOR to unconventional rock characterization and LWD-NMR field operations. The main strengths are the wide topical scope and the inclusion of many representative studies and technical concepts. However, the manuscript is substantially weakened by descriptive, paper-by-paper presentation, limited analytical synthesis, and frequent editorial, equation, citation, and cross-referencing problems that reduce the reliability and readability of the review.

---

### 1. Coverage

**Score: 3**

**Critical observations:**  
The survey covers many major areas within its declared scope: NMR theory, 1D/2D methods, laboratory petrophysics, EOR applications, drilling-fluid damage, emulsions, unconventional rock characterization, and field/LWD applications. However, coverage is uneven and often shallow, with some areas listed rather than meaningfully developed.

**Evidence:**  
- Laboratory petrophysics is well represented through porosity, PSD, permeability, saturation, capillary pressure, and wettability.
- The field-scale portion focuses heavily on LWD tool design and geosteering case studies, while broader wireline NMR formation-evaluation applications are less developed.
- Several topics, such as porosity studies in Table 3, are summarized mainly as study-by-study entries rather than integrated discussions.
- The breadth is appropriate to the declared scope, but the depth and balance are inconsistent.

---

### 2. Relevance

**Score: 4**

**Critical observations:**  
Virtually all substantive sections support the stated objective of reviewing NMR applications in oil and gas. Background material on NMR theory, relaxation, diffusion, and 2D methods is necessary for the survey and is generally tied to later applications. Occasional digressions, such as lithium-ion battery electrolytes and medical MRI, are brief and used to contextualize NMR instrumentation.

**Evidence:**  
- The introduction describes NMR instrumentation categories and connects them back to oilfield applications.
- The NMR theory section provides foundational concepts needed for later discussions.
- Some generic background is present, but it does not dominate the survey or replace domain-specific review content.

---

### 3. Structure

**Score: 3**

**Critical observations:**  
The overall organization is logical: theory, laboratory applications, special topics, field applications, future directions, and conclusions. However, transitions are often weak, some sections function as miscellaneous groupings, and there are repeated or broken section/figure references that disrupt progression.

**Evidence:**  
- “Special topics” combines drilling-fluid damage, emulsion sizing, misinterpretation in conventional rocks, and unconventional characterization with limited conceptual linkage.
- The heading “NMR applications in field scaleNMR technology has been widely used...” contains obvious duplication and indicates poor structural editing.
- Multiple “Error! Reference source not found” placeholders break the reading flow.
- The field-scale section is heavily paper-by-paper, which weakens conceptual progression.

---

### 4. Synthesis

**Score: 2**

**Critical observations:**  
The survey is primarily descriptive. It groups works by topic, but it rarely develops meaningful comparisons, trade-offs, trends, or conceptual frameworks. Many sections summarize individual studies sequentially rather than integrating them into broader insights.

**Evidence:**  
- The EOR section reports studies by Mitchell et al., Al Harbi et al., Dong et al., Mamoudou et al., and Zhu et al. largely independently, without a unified comparative analysis.
- Table 2 is essentially a list of applications and references rather than a synthesis.
- Table 4 is mentioned in the wettability section but is not actually presented, leaving the claimed summary unavailable.
- Some conceptual discussion exists for pore coupling, internal gradients, and unconventional fluid typing, but it is limited and uneven.

---

### 5. Accuracy & Evidence

**Score: 2**

**Critical observations:**  
The survey contains several substantive internal inconsistencies, technical errors, and unsupported comparative claims. Some equations are misnumbered or mathematically inconsistent with adjacent equations, and some important claims lack support.

**Evidence:**  
- Eq. 21 states \( \frac{1}{T_2} \approx \rho_2^2 \frac{S}{V} \), while Eq. 20 and the surrounding discussion use \( \frac{1}{T_2} \approx \rho_2 \frac{S}{V} \). This is an internal mathematical inconsistency.
- In the diffusion section, the text refers to “Eq. 8” for gradient-strength-dependent attenuation, but Eq. 8 defines phase shift, not signal attenuation. The equation numbering also appears out of order, with Eq. 12 introduced before Eqs. 10–11.
- The sentence “A successful attempt was achieved to capture solids and semi-solid NMR which have extremely short T2 (provide reference here please)” explicitly lacks required support.
- The abstract claims NMR was “found to be better than the current techniques used for screening, evaluation, and assessment,” but the survey does not provide systematic comparative evidence for this broad claim.
- The text promises Table 4 as a summary of NMR wettability indices, but no such table is included.

### 6. Citation Integrity

**Score: 2**

**Critical observations:**  
The survey shows multiple evident citation and reference-list problems, including missing citations, duplicate references, and inconsistent in-text labels. These are visible from the survey itself and do not require external verification.

**Evidence:**  
- A direct placeholder appears in the text: “provide reference here please.”
- Duplicate references are present, for example, two nearly identical entries for Sjöblom et al. (2017).
- In-text citation labels such as “Adebayo and Bageri 2020a/b” or “Bageri et al. 2021a/b” do not clearly correspond to distinct entries in the reference list.
- Several references appear merged or poorly delimited, e.g., entries run together such as “Cai et al. ... Fuel 108:292-302. https://doi.org/... Callaghan PT (2011) Translational Dynamics...”
- Broken cross-references to figures and tables further indicate poor citation and reference management.

---

### 7. Writing Quality & Editorial Consistency

**Score: 2**

**Critical observations:**  
The manuscript contains frequent typographical, terminological, and formatting inconsistencies that impair readability and suggest an incomplete editorial process. The writing is understandable in places, but the cumulative number of errors is substantial.

**Evidence:**  
- Frequent terminology inconsistencies: \(T_f\), \(T_1\), and \(T_j\) are used interchangeably in different places.
- Obvious typos: “filed” for “field,” “verity” for “variety,” “adsorped,” “restauration,” “pervious,” “trail,” and “CMPG” instead of “CPMG.”
- Broken figure references such as “Error! Reference source not found” appear multiple times.
- The heading “NMR applications in field scaleNMR technology...” contains a missing space and repeated text.
- Reference-list formatting is inconsistent, with duplicated entries and merged references.
- Table 4 is referenced but absent, and several figures appear to be discussed using placeholders rather than proper figure numbers.
